Metrology Gallery

Eight worked calibrations, in order of increasing difficulty. Each one exists for a conclusion that the algebra makes visible and that a single number does not — where the money should go, why a matched pair beats two good resistors, why a budget computed at one point does not describe the instrument.

Every example here is asserted end to end in test/gallery/.

1. Torque transducer — M = F·L

A known mass on a lever arm. Two inputs, one product, and the relative form of JCGM 100:2008 §5.1.6 equation (12) exactly: u(M)/M = √((u(F)/F)² + (u(L)/L)²).

using Symbolics, SymbolicUncertainties
@variables F σF L σL

M = SymbolicMeasurement(F, σF) * SymbolicMeasurement(L, σL)
vals = Dict(F => 98.0665, σF => 0.0015, L => 0.500, σL => 0.00005)
uncertainty_budget(M)
Uncertainty budget (EA-4/02 §7.3) for y = F*L
  F:  u = σF   c = L   |c|u = abs(L)*σF
  L:  u = σL   c = F   |c|u = abs(F)*σL
  u_c = sqrt((F^2)*(σL^2) + (L^2)*(σF^2))

The arm's relative uncertainty is 1e-4 against 1.5e-5 for the force, so it contributes five times more. Halving it nearly halves u_c; halving the force's barely moves it. That is the entire purpose of computing a budget rather than a number.

2. Four-wire Kelvin measurement

Measuring a milliohm shunt two-wire fails because the lead resistance is comparable to the resistand. Four-wire separates current injection from voltage sensing.

Correcting a two-wire reading for a known lead resistance is not equivalent: it leaves that correction's own uncertainty in the budget, where it dominates by twenty to one. The four-wire model carries no lead source at all — the budget shows the difference as a missing row, not as a small number. A change of measurement model, in the sense of JCGM GUM-6:2020, not a change of value.

3. Pressure transducer — two-point calibration

p(r) = (r − r₀)·P/(r₁ − r₀), from a zero and a span point.

The budget is not a property of the instrument. Near the bottom of the range the zero point dominates the two calibration constants; near the top the span reading and the reference standard do. The relative uncertainty is five times worse at the bottom, which is the physical reason transducers are specified over a stated turndown rather than over their full span.

4. Wheatstone bridge — matched ratio arms

At balance, Rx = R₃·(R₁/R₂).

A matched pair of ratio arms is far better than two independent resistors of the same tolerance, and the budget only says so if the correlation is declared. The arms enter as a ratio, so a positive correlation cancels — the mechanism of JCGM 100:2008 §H.4, where correlated counting rates reduce the combined uncertainty.

@variables R1 σR1 R2 σR2 R3 σR3
a, b = declare_correlated(
    SymbolicMeasurement(R1, σR1),
    SymbolicMeasurement(R2, σR2),
    1.0,
)
perfect = SymbolicMeasurement(R3, σR3) * (a / b)

At ρ = 0.9 the ratio's contribution falls by more than half. At ρ = 1 with equal relative tolerances it vanishes: only the standard is left. That is why bridges are built this way, and an uncorrelated budget cannot see it.

5. Pt100 — Callendar–van Dusen

The standard gives resistance as a function of temperature, R(t) = R₀(1 + A·t + B·t²), while the measurand is the temperature. The model is the inverse, and its sensitivity coefficient is the reciprocal of the direct slope, ∂t/∂R = 1/(R₀(A + 2B·t)).

Because B is negative that slope falls as temperature rises: the same 10 mΩ is worth 26 m°C at 0 °C and appreciably more at 600 °C. A budget computed once, at one point, does not describe the instrument.

6. Thermocouple with cold-junction compensation

A thermocouple measures a difference, so the reading must be referred back to 0 °C before the ITS-90 relation can be inverted: E(T) = E_measured + E(T_cj).

The cold junction enters through the Seebeck coefficient at its own temperature, which is comparable to the one at the hot junction. A cold junction known to 0.5 °C therefore puts about 0.47 °C into the measurand — ten times the voltmeter's contribution. Ten times better voltage resolution changes nothing; ten times better cold-junction sensing divides u_c by five. The budget says which to buy, before the money is spent.

7. Interferometric displacement — the air, not the laser

d = N·λ/(2n), with n from the simplified Edlén form.

A stabilised HeNe is good to 1e-8 relative — 10 nm over a metre. The refractive index of air moves by about 1e-6 per °C and per 300 Pa. Even at 0.1 °C and 50 Pa the air contributes 93 nm and 134 nm against the laser's 10; in room conditions it wins by four orders of magnitude. A ten-times better laser changes nothing measurable.

8. Gauge R&R — where ISO 5725 stops

A gauge R&R study decomposes the observed scatter into repeatability and reproducibility. It therefore yields Type A components only: the Type B terms — calibration of the reference, finite resolution, a systematic offset — do not vary between repeats and no amount of repeating reveals them.

Two consequences. The reported u_c is understated, here by 7 %. And the coverage factor is inflated, because three operators give the reproducibility term two degrees of freedom: Welch-Satterthwaite lets that dominate, ν_eff falls well below the naive 30, and k exceeds 2.1. Ten operators would barely change u_c and would tighten the interval appreciably — an experiment-design conclusion the budget hands you for free.
